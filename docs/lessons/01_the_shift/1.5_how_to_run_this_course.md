### 1.5 How to Run This Course

Courses don't fail because the material is too hard. They fail because life is busy and there was no system. This lesson gives you the system: the rhythm, the journal, the lane discipline, and the honest expectations that get you from here to a shipped capstone.

!!! abstract "What you will learn"
    - Set up the builder's journal you'll keep for the whole course.
    - Choose your lane and know when to switch.
    - Plan a realistic schedule you'll actually keep.
    - Know exactly what to do when you get stuck.

!!! success "Builder principle"
    Consistency beats intensity. One focused hour, four times a week, finishes this course. One heroic Saturday doesn't.

## The builder's journal

Before anything else, create one place where the whole journey lives. Notes app, paper notebook, doc — the medium doesn't matter; the habit does.

It already has three entries if you've done the lessons so far: your time-eater list (1.1), your chatbot-vs-agent brief (1.2), and your three builds (1.4). From here, add after every lesson:

- **What I did** — one line.
- **What surprised me** — one line.
- **What I'd try next** — one line.

Three lines. Thirty seconds. By Chapter 11 this journal becomes something unexpected: the raw material for your capstone build log, your portfolio story, and — if you go the client route — proof of process that closes deals.

!!! tip "Meta move"
    In Chapter 5 you'll learn to make your journal *agent-readable*, turning it into memory your AI can use. Write it like a future teammate will read it. One will.

## Choose your lane — and hold it

=== "🌱 No-code lane"

    Read every lesson fully. Do every "Try it now." Watch the linked videos — they're chosen specifically to replace the background a CS degree would have given you, one hour at a time.

    **Your risk:** discouragement in Chapter 3 when the terminal is new. Plan for it now: that chapter is deliberately slower, and the discomfort is one week long. Everyone who pushed through says the same thing — it was smaller than it looked.

=== "⚙️ Engineer lane"

    Skim Track 1 in an evening — but actually do the 1.4 build-list exercise; vague targets sink senior people too. Slow down hard at Chapter 4. The chapters that will change how you work are 5, 7, 8, 9, and 10.

    **Your risk:** arrogance in Chapters 4–5. "I already use AI" is not the same as running a disciplined agentic loop with engineered context. The difference shows up in output quality — measure yours before and after.

Switch lanes any time. Reading the other lane's tabs is encouraged — knowing how the other half works is part of the skill.

## The schedule that works

| Your reality | The plan | Course finish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full-time job + life | 3–4 hours/week (two evenings) | ~12 weeks |
| Grinding for a switch | 8–10 hours/week | ~5 weeks |
| Engineer skimming Track 1 | 5 hours/week | ~6 weeks |

Rules that keep people finishing:

1. **Schedule the sessions.** Calendar, recurring, non-negotiable — like a class you paid for. (You did pay: with attention.)
2. **End every session mid-momentum.** Stop while you know your next step. Blank-page starts kill streaks.
3. **Never miss twice.** Miss a session? Fine. Missing two in a row is a decision — make it consciously or don't make it.

## When you get stuck

You will get stuck. Here's the order of operations:

1. **Read the error out loud.** Half of all stuck-ness dissolves right here.
2. **Check the [Glossary](../../course/glossary.md).** Unfamiliar words masquerade as hard problems.
3. **Ask an AI.** Seriously — paste the error and what you were trying to do into Claude. Learning to get unstuck *with* AI is course material, not cheating.
4. **Step away for ten minutes.** The shower has debugged more problems than any tool ever made.
5. **Ask the community.** [GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/thetechhustle/build_with_ai/discussions) — someone has hit your wall before, and your question helps the next person.
