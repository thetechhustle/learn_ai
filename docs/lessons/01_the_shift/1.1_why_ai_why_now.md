### 1.1 Why AI, Why Now

You've heard "AI is changing everything" so many times it's become wallpaper. This lesson strips the hype and shows you the three concrete things that changed — because when you can name what changed, you can position yourself on the right side of it.

!!! abstract "What you will learn"
    - Name the three shifts that made this moment different from every previous AI hype cycle.
    - Explain why the cost of building software collapsed — and what that means for who gets to build.
    - Recognize the difference between AI hype and AI leverage.

!!! success "Builder principle"
    Waves reward position, not perfection. You don't need to be the best — you need to be early and consistent.

## What actually changed

AI has been "five years away" since the 1960s. Three things made this time different:

**1. The models got general.** Older AI did one thing — recognize a face, recommend a song. Large language models do *whatever you can describe in words*: write, summarize, plan, code, analyze, translate. One tool, every domain. That has never existed before.

**2. The models got hands.** Until recently, AI could only talk. Now agents can *act* — read and write real files, run real commands, use real tools, check their own work. The gap between "AI gave me advice" and "AI did the task" is the entire shift, and it happened in roughly the last two years.

**3. The price collapsed.** Work that cost a $150/hour developer now costs cents of compute. Not because developers got cheaper — because a new kind of labor entered the market. When the price of making software drops a hundredfold, the question stops being "can I afford to build it?" and becomes "what should I build?"

## Why this matters to you specifically

Every previous tech wave had a gate. The internet needed you to learn HTML. Mobile needed you to learn Swift or Java. App stores needed capital and connections.

This wave's gate is *language*. If you can describe what you want clearly — and verify what you got honestly — you can build. That's a skill issue, not a credential issue. And skills can be trained, which is literally what this course is.

The people most rewarded in the next five years won't be the ones who "know AI trivia." They'll be the ones with the *habit* of turning ideas into shipped things faster than the people around them. Speed of execution is the new moat.

## Hype vs. leverage

Not everything with "AI" on the label deserves your time. A working filter:

| Hype | Leverage |
| --- | --- |
| "AI will replace everyone" | "AI moves specific tasks from hours to minutes — which of *my* tasks?" |
| Buying courses about prompts | Shipping one real project this month |
| Arguing about AGI on the internet | Automating your own Monday morning |
| Waiting for the tools to "settle down" | Learning the concepts that survive every tool change |

This course lives entirely in the right column. The concepts you'll learn — the agentic loop, context engineering, verification, orchestration — transfer across every tool, because they're about *how you direct machine work*, not which button to press.

## The honest caveats

Real leverage requires real honesty:

- Models confidently make things up (Chapter 2 covers why, and what to do about it).
- Agents can act wrongly as easily as they act rightly — which is why *verify* is a permanent step in your loop, never an optional one.
- The tools change fast. The fundamentals don't. We teach fundamentals through current tools.
