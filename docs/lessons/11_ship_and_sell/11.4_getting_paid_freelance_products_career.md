### 11.4 Getting Paid: Freelance, Products, Career

Everything converges here. You have shipped work with URLs, a harness that halves your delivery time, unit economics on paper, and a journal full of receipts. This lesson turns the stack into income through the three doors — freelance, products, career — with honest first steps for each, and the packaging move that makes all three easier: proof over promises.

!!! abstract "What you will learn"
    - Choose your door — freelance, products, or career leverage — based on your actual situation.
    - Land a first paid project with the receipts-first approach.
    - Package your proof: the portfolio that closes without you in the room.

!!! success "Builder principle"
    Nobody buys "I know AI." Everybody buys "here's one I made for someone like you — click it." Sell the click.

## Door one: freelance

The fastest money, because the market inefficiency is enormous and local: every barber, bakery, trainer, church, and side hustle around you needs what Chapter 6 taught you to make in a weekend — and they're currently quoted thousands or ghosted entirely.

**The first-client playbook, receipts-first:**

1. **Build one free — strategically.** Someone in your circle with a real business and no site. Full 6.2 recipe, full quality bar, custom domain (11.1). In exchange: permission to show it, a testimonial, and referrals. This isn't working free; it's buying your proof at cost — the strongest marketing asset in existence.
2. **Price from your unit economics** (11.3), not from fear: local market for a solid small-business site runs $500–1,500 — at your cost of goods, healthy margin lives anywhere in that range. Beginners underprice out of impostor feelings; your receipts are the antidote — you *know* what it costs you to deliver.
3. **Sell the run, not just the build** — 11.2 was the retainer curriculum, remember: hosting + monitoring + monthly maintenance + small changes at $50–150/month. Five retainer clients is a meaningful income floor, and your `/health` routine services all five in an hour on Mondays.
4. **Deliver like the professional you now are:** discovery file → previews mid-build (clients *love* preview URLs) → the HANDOFF.md → the launch checklist including the announcement. Your process — visible — is what justifies your price and generates the referral.

## Door two: products

Slower, more leveraged: build once, sell repeatedly. Templates, automation packs, niche tools, paid content built on your second-brain workflows — and the meta-product hiding in your repo since Chapter 7: **your Skill library is a product** (7.4 called it; the market agrees — packaged expertise for a niche sells).

The honest sequencing: products need distribution — an audience, a niche presence, or patience for slow organic growth. The standard path runs *through* door one: freelance in a niche → notice what every client needs → productize that thing → your client work becomes your product research. Skipping straight to products with no audience is the most common way builders stall; go through the doors in order unless you already have distribution.

## Door three: career

The compounding door, and the one for everyone who *likes* their field: agentic skills applied inside your current career. The market signal you researched in 1.3 has only strengthened — and here's your structural advantage: **most workplaces are still at Chapter 2.** You're arriving with Chapter 10.

- **Become the person visibly.** Automate a team pain point (6.5), demo the before/after honestly (including cost — 11.3 gave you the language), and share the *how*. Hoarding the technique makes you a curiosity; teaching it makes you a leader. Guess which gets promoted.
- **Bring artifacts, not vibes.** The harness for your team's repo (5.6's Monday move; 10.4's platform framing), the shared Skill library, the reviewer subagent on PRs — these are *demonstrable infrastructure*, the kind of contribution that shows up in reviews and interviews both.
- **Interview with receipts.** "Experience with AI-assisted development" on the posting; your capstone URL, your harness repo, and a five-minute walkthrough of your multi-agent workflow in the interview. That conversation currently separates you from a startling percentage of candidates who list the buzzword.

## The packaging: proof over promises

Whichever door, the asset is the same — a portfolio built for the thirty-second visit:

```
yourname.com            ← 11.1's domain, of course
├── 3-5 shipped things  ← LIVE LINKS first, screenshots second,
│                          one line each: what + for whom + result
├── one process page    ← the 6.2 recipe as YOUR method, with a
│                          real build log excerpt (receipts, visible)
└── one honest line     ← who you are, what you build, how to reach you
```

Three shipped things beat thirty claimed skills. The build-log excerpt is the differentiator nobody else has — it shows *process*, and process is what buyers of all three doors are actually buying. You've been keeping the raw material since Lesson 1.5 (this was always the plan).

=== "🌱 No-code lane"

    The impostor feeling around charging money — name it and then check it against evidence: you have deployed URLs, a documented process, production habits, and cost-of-goods numbers. That's *more* operational maturity than a large share of people currently charging for this work. Start at door one, strategic freebie this month, first paid job priced from receipts next month. The culture doesn't need you humble; it needs you *paid* — and then it needs you putting someone else on, which is 11.5's business.

=== "⚙️ Engineer lane"

    Doors one and two are open to you too (weekend consulting at agentic speed is quietly lucrative), but door three compounds hardest for you: the org that watches you ship with a 10.4 harness while peers debate prompts is an org where your next level writes itself. The step most engineers skip: *make the invisible visible* — internal demo, written playbook, lunch-and-learn. Your multiplier was never just your output; it's your output × everyone you bring along. That's also, not coincidentally, how staff-level cases get made.

## Try it now

1. Choose your door — in the journal, with the *why* (situation, not aspiration: distribution? day job you like? need money soonest?).
2. Door one: identify the strategic freebie and pitch them this week (the pitch is one sentence: "I'll build your site free; you give me a testimonial and referrals"). Door three: pick the team pain point and schedule the demo.
3. Build the portfolio page — you have the pieces (builds, logs, domain, recipe). One evening, agent-assisted, obviously.
4. Set the revenue goal with a date: first paid project / first product sale / the internal demo delivered — 30 days. Write it where future-you will find it. Future-you has a good track record with your journal entries by now.
