### 11.1 Launch: Beyond GitHub Pages

GitHub Pages got your static sites live for free — and for a surprising share of real work, it's all you'll ever need. But the moment a project wants a backend, a database, a custom domain, or a form that actually *sends*, you're choosing from the modern hosting landscape. Good news: it's friendlier and cheaper than its jargon suggests, and your agent does the heavy lifting.

!!! abstract "What you will learn"
    - Map the hosting tiers: static, serverless platforms, and when you'd need more.
    - Launch on a modern platform (Vercel/Netlify class) with a custom domain.
    - Run the launch checklist that separates "live" from "launched."

!!! success "Builder principle"
    Match the platform to the project, not to the hype. The right answer is usually the boring one with the generous free tier.

## The tiers, demystified

**Tier 1 — Static hosting** (GitHub Pages, and the static side of Netlify/Vercel/Cloudflare Pages). Files served as-is: sites, portfolios, landing pages, docs — everything you've shipped so far. Free, fast, nearly unbreakable. *Stay here as long as you can.*

**Tier 2 — Serverless platforms** (Vercel, Netlify, Cloudflare). Static hosting *plus* small pieces of on-demand backend (form handling, API routes, auth) and niceties: deploy-on-push, preview URLs per branch, custom domains with automatic HTTPS. Free tiers cover most solo and small-client work. This tier is where most of your Chapter 6 recipe clients graduate to, and where full apps (the engineer lane's Next.js builds and friends) live comfortably.

**Tier 3 — Real infrastructure** (VPS, containers, managed cloud). For when you have persistent processes, big databases, or traffic that outgrows tier 2. If you genuinely need this, you'll know — and the same delegation pattern applies, so we won't pretend it needs its own course. Rule of thumb: *being scared of tier 3 is fine; reaching for it out of vanity is expensive.*

## The launch, delegated

Platform choice made (your agent can brief the tradeoffs for *your* specific project — ask), the launch itself is an agent task with human gates exactly where you'd expect:

```text
This project is ready to launch on [platform]. Set it up:
deploy config, connect the GitHub repo for deploy-on-push, and
walk me through the account/domain steps you can't do — one at
a time, telling me what each does.
Done means: the site is live on its platform URL, a test change
deploys automatically when pushed, and you've shown me the
preview-URL flow for branches.
```

Two notes on what just happened. **Deploy-on-push** is your Git rhythm (3.3) growing its final limb — commit, push, *live* — with your harness and hooks (9) standing between you and shipping breakage. And **preview URLs per branch** quietly upgrade your whole Chapter 10 practice: every team lane's PR now carries a *clickable live preview* — your acceptance pass (4.4) and reviewer subagent can inspect real deployed behavior per lane. The stack keeps composing, right to the end.

**The custom domain** — the $10–15/year upgrade that changes how work is perceived: `yourname.com` vs. a platform subdomain is the difference between a business card and a name tag. Buy from any reputable registrar; the agent walks you through DNS (two record changes, ten minutes, feels like sorcery the first time, is plumbing forever after).

## Live ≠ launched

The checklist that separates the two — run it on everything you ship from now on:

- [ ] **The basics read right:** real page title, favicon, social-preview card (test by pasting your URL in a chat — that unfurl is your first impression at scale).
- [ ] **Someone else's device:** the 4.4 pass, on hardware you don't own. Non-negotiable, forever.
- [ ] **The form actually arrives** (if there's a form): submit it, receive it. "Deployed" and "delivers" are different claims.
- [ ] **Something watches it:** at minimum, an uptime monitor pinging the URL (free tiers abound) so a dead site tells *you* before it tells a client. (11.2 goes deeper.)
- [ ] **The announcement:** launched means *told people*. One post, one message to the person it's for, one journal entry with the URL. Work that nobody knows about is inventory, not shipping.

=== "🌱 No-code lane"

    Tier 2's free tier + a custom domain is your professional-grade setup — genuinely the same stack solo devs charge real money on top of. Do the graduation this week: take your best Chapter 6 build, move it to Vercel or Netlify (the agent makes it painless), put a real domain on it, run the checklist. Total cost: the domain. Total upgrade in how it reads to a paying client: substantial.

=== "⚙️ Engineer lane"

    Nothing here surprised you — so make the *delegation* the exercise: full launch, agent-driven, you touching only the account/DNS gates, and note where your habits made you grab the wheel unnecessarily. Then the good stuff: preview-URLs-per-lane wired into your 10.3 team template (reviewer subagent gets the preview link in its brief), and deploy gates as the final PostToolUse frontier — harness green *required* before push-to-main means "it can't deploy broken" is mechanism, not aspiration (9.1, closing its own loop).
